# Human-Centred Explainable AI for Social Media-Based Behavioural Risk Detection

## Overview
This research investigates human-centred explainable AI (XAI) for analysing relationships between social-media usage behaviours and self-reported psychological wellbeing. It was developed as part of an MSc research project and published as an IGI Global book chapter:

**“Human-Centred Explainable AI for Social Media-Based Behavioural Risk Detection in Digital Mental Health Systems”**

## Data and methods
The study used survey data from 1,491 participants. Logistic Regression and XGBoost were applied, with reported ROC-AUC values of approximately 0.865 and 0.866, respectively. SHAP was used to support model explainability.

The research considered how explanations may support human stakeholders and discussed responsible-AI principles including fairness, informed consent, non-maleficence, human oversight, auditability, and ongoing monitoring.

## Research contribution
The work combined predictive modelling and explainability with a human-centred perspective on responsible AI in digital mental-health-related systems.

## Responsible use and limitations
- The data were cross-sectional and self-reported; the findings do not establish causal relationships.
- This work should not be interpreted as a clinical diagnostic tool or as evidence of clinical effectiveness.
- Longitudinal data and more objective measures of social-media usage are potential directions for future research.
- Do not describe a proposed framework or design recommendations as validated in real-world deployment unless supported by separate evaluation evidence.
- Handle participant data in accordance with applicable consent, ethics, and data-protection requirements. Do not publish identifiable or restricted participant-level data.
